MISSION BRIEFING

Glorious Purpose

To make a machine read your hands the way a person would — and to prove I understood every step of how it works, not just that it works.

Point at a part and it names itself. Pinch the air and the model is yours to turn. Pull your hands apart and the whole machine blooms into a labelled, exploded diagram floating in front of you. A hologram you can actually teach with.

The Idea

A hand-tracking model watches the webcam and hands back twenty-one points per hand. That is all it gives me — coordinates, nothing more.

Every gesture on top of that is geometry I wrote by hand and can explain line by line: what distance makes a pinch a pinch, how a drifting palm becomes rotation, how the gap between two hands becomes zoom. The model is a sensor. The interaction is mine.

No language model, no server, no API key, no build step — it opens as one web page and talks to your camera. The hard part was never calling a library. It was tuning the maths against a real, shaky, badly-lit webcam until the model felt like it was actually in your hands.
